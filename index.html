Mindclip is a YouTube channel of short clips from long lectures and talks. Each clip holds one complete idea, and points you to the lecture it came from.

Why the channel exists

A good lecture is long because an argument needs room. But inside it there are moments where one idea arrives whole: a definition, a distinction, a story that makes the point. Those moments stand on their own, and most people never reach them, because nobody has two hours to spare on a guess.

Mindclip finds those moments and posts them by themselves. If one of them stays with you, the full lecture is named in the description, and that is where the idea is argued properly.

What a clip is

  • One idea, start to finish

    A clip begins where the thought begins and ends where it lands. No cliffhangers, and no cutting away before the point is made.

  • The speaker's words, in the speaker's order

    Pauses, repeats and asides are trimmed to fit the length. Nothing is rearranged or joined up to say something the speaker did not say.

  • Captioned, every time

    Every clip carries word-by-word captions, so it works with the sound off and for viewers who cannot hear it.

  • The source is named

    Each description says whose lecture the clip came from, so you can go and watch the whole thing.

How clips are made

  1. Software transcribes a publicly available lecture, word by word.

  2. An AI model reads the transcript and marks the passages where a complete idea is made, from its first sentence to its last.

  3. Each passage is cut out, framed upright on the speaker and captioned.

  4. The clip is uploaded to the Mindclip channel with a title that says what it is about and a line naming the full lecture.

The choosing and cutting are done by software, including AI, and not by an editor watching every minute. That is how one person can run the channel. It also means a clip can occasionally miss; if you see one that misrepresents a speaker, say so and it will be taken down.

For speakers and rights holders

Mindclip is an independent channel. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by any of the people who appear in its clips.

If you are the speaker in a clip, or you hold the rights to a lecture, and you want a clip removed or credited differently, write to nraclips@gmail.com with a link to the clip. It will be removed or corrected.

The uploading tool

Clips reach YouTube through a small program called short-clipper, which runs on the channel owner's own computer. It signs in to the channel owner's YouTube account to upload clips and to read the channel's own view counts. Nobody else signs in to it, and it is not offered to the public.

What it can see, what it keeps and how to switch it off are set out in the privacy policy.
